About

Most investors don’t need more products. They need a clearer view of the ones already in front of them. At MY Securities I sit between investment products, advisors, and investors — researching markets, evaluating new opportunities across alternatives and private markets, and translating intricate structures into language people can act on.

That work is equal parts diligence and education. I spend as much time asking what a product does in a bad year as on what it promises in a good one, and just as much time making sure the advisors and clients who hold it understand the difference. Clarity, in this business, is a form of risk management.

I hold the FINRA Series 65 and the SIE, and a bachelor’s degree from the University of Southern California. I came to finance by way of media production — years spent managing budgets, timelines, and cross-functional teams against deadlines that could not move. It is an unusual route, and a useful one: it taught me that every detail is structural, and that complicated things can be made legible without being made simplistic.

Approach

A structure nobody can explain is a structure nobody should own. My job is to make sure the explanation exists — and that it is honest about what the product does and does not do.

Research before conviction

Diligence starts with the downside. I want to understand the mechanics, the fee structure, and the failure cases before I form a view on the opportunity.

Fit before product

Differentiated does not mean appropriate. The question is never only whether a solution is good, but whose objectives it actually serves.

Plain language, always

Complexity in a structure is often necessary. Complexity in the explanation of it rarely is.
